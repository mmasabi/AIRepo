What each level demands from avionics

One family of scalable building blocks, not one computer

Capability Class I: small UAV and loitering munition Class II: tactical UAV Class III and manned aircraft
Flight control (FCU) Integrated, low-SWaP autopilot, single channel Dedicated FCU with dual redundancy Redundant flight-critical computing (dual or triplex)
Mission computing (MCU) Lightweight companion computer, video and AI at the edge Dedicated mission computer for EO/IR, sensors and datalinks High-performance modular MCU, sensor fusion, SATCOM, AI
Stores management (SMS) Safe-arm-fire logic for loitering munitions, otherwise none Light SMS for a few small munitions, safety interlocks Full SMS: many stations, MIL-STD-1760 interfaces, release sequencing
Software and assurance COTS-based, rapid iteration Structured process to STANAG 4671 airworthiness DO-178C / DO-254 at high assurance levels, cybersecurity

What the Center will do

From individual products to an integrated portfolio

Domain Responsibility

Proposed operating model

A federated Center of Excellence across EDGE

Model 1

ADASI-led

ADASI leads; other entities join through collaboration agreements.

+ Builds on existing ADASI products

− May be seen as an ADASI initiative

Model 2

EDGE-level

A group-level Center with its own mandate and governance.

+ Strong cross-entity authority

− More organizational change and sponsorship

Model 3 · Recommended

Federated

The Center sets technology roadmaps, architecture standards, common IP strategy and engineering governance.

ADASI, HALCON and EDGE platform entities keep program execution and platform responsibility.

A conceptual operating model, not a proposed reporting structure. Final reporting lines are for executive agreement.

Implementation roadmap

A phased approach to building the capability

0–3 months

1 · Assess and align

Map existing capabilities, identify overlaps, define governance and initial product scope

3–6 months

2 · Establish

Approve the charter, assign domain leads, define the technology roadmap and shared standards

6–12 months

3 · Demonstrate

Select pilot programs, demonstrate reusable architecture across mission levels, measure initial benefits

12–24 months

4 · Scale

Expand product families toward Class II and Class III, mature common technologies, explore commercialization

The first six months focus on capability alignment and demonstrable value, not organizational expansion.
